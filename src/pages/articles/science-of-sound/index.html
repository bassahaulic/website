The Science of Sound: Why Phase and Power Outweigh "Output"

In the world of custom enclosure design, there is a growing trend toward measuring Cabin Gain—the natural acoustic boost a vehicle provides. While understanding this gain is a step in the right direction, many "elite" services rely on a simplified "Delta" measurement that ignores the two most critical factors in high-fidelity audio: Electrical Impedance and Acoustic Phase.

At this level of performance, designing for "loudness" is easy. Designing for cohesion, timing, and accuracy is where the real engineering begins.

1 The Output Level Illusion

Many measurement kits work by comparing the output of a test box in a field versus its output inside a car. By subtracting one from the other, they create a "Delta" graph.

The Problem

This method assumes the amplifier is delivering the exact same amount of power in both environments. In reality, a subwoofer's Impedance changes based on the air pressure (loading) it faces inside a cabin. Without measuring the voltage and current at the speaker terminals, you aren't measuring the "Car"—you're measuring a changing electrical load.

You cannot accurately calculate acoustic "gain" if you don't know the exact "input" power.

2 Phase: The Hidden Dimension of Bass

Frequency response (how loud each note is) tells you what is happening. Acoustic Phase tells you when it is happening.

In a vehicle, sound waves from your subwoofer reflect off glass, plastic, and metal, rotating the phase of the wave. If your enclosure design ignores this rotation:

  • Mid-Bass Cancellation: Your subwoofer and door speakers may be "out of step" at the crossover point. Even if your RTA looks "flat," the speakers are fighting each other, creating a "hollow" sound that no amount of EQ can fix.
  • Sluggish Response: High "Group Delay" makes bass feel like it's lagging behind the beat. This isn't a volume issue; it's a timing issue.

3 The "Front Stage" Handshake

The goal of a world-class system is for the bass to feel like it is originating from the dashboard, not the trunk. This "Front Stage" effect is only possible through precise phase alignment between every driver in the car.

A standard tablet-and-mic kit captures a snapshot of pressure. It does not capture the high-resolution timing data required to align a subwoofer with the rest of the audio system.
